TRAFFIC TSUNAMI: Large-Scale DDoS Crisis

Welcome, **SOC Analyst** at **CORESEC Technologies**! Your task is critical: your company's main servers are under a **large-scale DDoS attack** from an international botnet. Websites, services, and even internal network connections are on the verge of disruption. This attack threatens business continuity by overloading systems and is causing major damage to your reputation. Your mission: Race against time to mitigate the attack, redirect traffic, protect the infrastructure, and ensure business continuity!

Phase 1: Initial Detection and Traffic Anomaly

Time 0:00 - Traffic Alert!

At midnight, a series of critical alerts start coming from your network monitoring systems: "Anomalous traffic increase detected!" You quickly realize that a distributed and intense flood of requests, tens of times larger than normal traffic, is being directed at your main servers. This is a clear **large-scale DDoS attack**! Your website is starting to slow down, and some users are already experiencing access issues.

Learning Note: In DDoS attacks, the speed of the initial response is vital. Using your own existing capacity can be a quick solution, while ISP support is necessary for larger attacks.

Phase 2: Contacting the CDN/DDoS Protection Provider

Time 0:45 - Response from the CDN Provider!

Although your on-prem solutions have provided some relief, the magnitude of the attack persists. With approval from the CISO and CTO, you've initiated an emergency call with your CDN and DDoS protection provider (e.g., Cloudflare, Akamai). You've detailed the situation. The provider states they can fully activate their traffic scrubbing service, but full activation and traffic redirection will take **approximately 15-30 minutes**.

What will you do to ensure business continuity during this waiting period?

Learning Note: The activation time for external DDoS mitigation solutions is important. Managing internal resources during this period is critical to minimizing downtime.

Phase 3: Traffic Diversion and Damage Control

Time 1:30 - Traffic Scrubbing Has Begun!

Your CDN provider has stepped in and started scrubbing the attack traffic. The initial signs are positive; system load is slowly decreasing. However, the attackers seem to have changed tactics, focusing on Layer 7 (application layer) attacks, targeting specific API endpoints and session management. This is a smarter type of attack that focuses on resource exhaustion rather than bandwidth.

CTO Onur Güngor calls: *"The attack volume is down, but we still have access issues! The APIs have slowed down. We're still getting complaints from our customers. What are we doing about this Layer 7 attack?"*

Learning Note: Layer 7 DDoS attacks are more sophisticated and cause service outages through resource exhaustion. Application-layer measures like rate limiting and CAPTCHA are important against such attacks.

Phase 4: Communication and Reputation Management

Time 2:30 - Media and Customer Pressure!

The impact of the DDoS attack has become public. Complaints are soaring on social media, and some news sites are headlining CORESEC Technologies' service outages. With a major e-commerce launch scheduled for this week, CEO Ayfer Yıldız is furious: *"This jeopardizes the launch! Our trust is eroding. What are we going to tell our customers? What will our financial losses be?"*

The PR team and your legal advisors are requesting an emergency meeting on the content and timing of the public statement.

Learning Note: In cybersecurity incidents, the communication strategy directly impacts reputation and customer trust. Transparency is often the best approach.

Phase 5: Post-Attack Recovery and Lessons Learned

Time 3:30 - Attack Under Control!

Thanks to the full support of your CDN provider and your proactive interventions, the DDoS attack is finally under control. Traffic has returned to normal levels, and your services are fully accessible. However, it's time to assess the impact of the attack and strengthen your infrastructure against potential future attacks. You are holding a post-crisis meeting with the CISO, CTO, and CEO.

With the lessons learned from this major DDoS attack, how will you strengthen your future defenses?

Learning Note: Lessons learned after a DDoS attack are critical for future resilience. Technology investments, training, and architectural changes provide long-term protection.

Phase 6: Investigation and Attribution

Time 4:30 - On the Attacker's Trail

The attack is completely stopped, and systems are stable. CISO Melih Kaan now wants you to focus on finding out who attacked and why. The forensic team has analyzed the attack vectors, source IPs, and traffic patterns, identifying three possible suspects.

Suspect 1: Disruptor Tech (Rival Company)

Motive: To sabotage CORESEC Technologies' major e-commerce launch and gain a market advantage.
Evidence: The attack was a sophisticated Layer 7 (application layer) attack specifically targeting API endpoints related to the new product. This might indicate they have inside knowledge of your company's operations.

Suspect 2: Digital Justice Front (Hacktivist Group)

Motive: To protest CORESEC Technologies' recently announced data policies, which have been controversial.
Evidence: During the attack, numerous posts criticizing your company were made from social media accounts associated with the group. The attack was high-volume but less complex, focusing on Layer 3/4 (network layer).

Suspect 3: Crypto-Lock Gang (Ransom Group)

Motive: Purely financial. To demand a ransom by taking the company's services offline.
Evidence: 24 hours before the attack, a low-profile email, which had been overlooked in the spam folder, demanded a payment of 5 BTC to "prevent disruptions to your services." The botnet used was seen in previous attacks associated with this group.

Now is the time to decide: In light of the evidence gathered, who do you believe is behind the attack? This decision will affect the legal process and future defense priorities.
