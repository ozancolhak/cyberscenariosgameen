Welcome to the Scenario

In this interactive scenario, you are a cybersecurity engineer at COLHAK Corporation. Your mission is to analyze a developing security threat, make critical decisions, and protect the company from a potential disaster. Carefully evaluate the information presented at each step. Are you ready?

Simulation Roles

  • Your Role: Cybersecurity Engineer - You are part of COLHAK Corporation's cybersecurity team. You will take an active role in incident response, perform analysis, and make critical decisions.
  • Ezgi (Tier-1 SOC Analyst) - The eyes and ears of your team; she is the one who detects initial alerts and suspicious activities.
  • Alper (Tier-2 Analyst) - Your specialist who analyzes incidents escalated from Ezgi in greater depth and conducts technical investigations.
  • Ceyda (Cybersecurity Manager) - As the leader of your cybersecurity team, she is the one who makes the final decisions and provides strategic guidance during a crisis.
  • Sales Director - A critical stakeholder responsible for the company's commercial operations; they absolutely do not want business processes like live demos to be disrupted.
  • Chief Financial Officer (CFO) - The senior executive responsible for the company's financial health.

🕒 07:15 – First Signal: Enriched SIEM Alert

At the start of the day, Tier-1 SOC Analyst Ezgi notices a critical alert in the SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) panel. A correlation rule has been triggered: "Periodic Data Exfiltration from Critical Asset Network to Unknown C2 Server".

ALERT ID: 8451
TRIGGER: Correlation Rule - "Suspicious C2 Beaconing"
SOURCE IP: 10.10.30.15 (VLAN: IoT Devices)
HOSTNAME: CAMERA-3FL-OCEAN
SOURCE MAC: 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E
DESTINATION IP: 88.54.12.219 (Reputation: Low, Category: Unknown)
PROTOCOL: UDP/443
OBSERVATION: A 128KB data packet has been sent every 5 minutes for the last 3 hours.
PCAP SUMMARY: DNS query for 'update.serve-config.net' resolved to 88.54.12.219. UDP payload contains obfuscated Base64 strings.
MITRE ATT&CK MAPPING: T1041 (Exfiltration Over C2), T1571 (Non-Standard Port)
Ezgi: "This isn't normal traffic. The source IP is on the IoT VLAN, and the destination is a suspicious address we haven't seen before. The MITRE mappings are also concerning. I'm escalating this to Tier-2 (Alper) immediately."

🕒 07:30 – Simultaneous Developments & The First Decision

Almost at the same time as Ezgi's analysis, two different event logs appear in the system. As a team, you must decide which event to prioritize.

Event 1: Help Desk Ticket #7254

User: Ayse Yilmaz

Subject: The camera in the 3rd Floor Ocean Conference Room is constantly freezing, and the web interface is very slow. There was no such problem yesterday morning.

Event 2: Critical System Alert #9112 (Red Herring)

System: Active Directory

Subject: Over 50 failed login attempts on the Chief Financial Officer (CFO)'s account have been detected in the last 15 minutes.

Team Lead: "We have two potentially serious incidents. One is a potential data leak, the other could be an attack on a senior executive's account. Our resources are limited, which one do we focus on?"

🕒 07:50 – Mis-Prioritization

The team decides to focus on the CFO's account. A quick investigation reveals that the CFO is on vacation abroad and was repeatedly trying to log in with the wrong password from his mobile device because he forgot the new one. The account is temporarily locked, and the situation is brought under control.

Alper: "It was a false positive. But we lost about 20 minutes investigating it. We need to get back to the other incident urgently. I hope we're not too late."

🧑‍💻 08:00 – Alper’s Deep-Dive Analysis

Tier-2 Analyst Alper correlates the SIEM alert with the help desk ticket. He queries the suspicious IP address `10.10.30.15` in the company's Asset Management System. The result is clear: the IP address belongs to a "SmartEye 3000" model IP camera in the 3rd Floor Ocean Conference Room.

A quick internet search reveals a recently disclosed critical vulnerability (CVE-2025-1337) in this model's firmware. This vulnerability allows for unauthorized Remote Code Execution (RCE).

Alper: "Okay, it all makes sense now. The slowness of the camera isn't a malfunction, it's a symptom. The device was compromised via a known vulnerability and is now part of a botnet, sending data to a C2 server. We need to decide on the next step."

🕵️‍♂️ 08:15 – Deepening the Investigation: What is the Attacker's Intent?

Alper's findings are clear: the device is compromised. But what is the enemy's real goal? Is it just a DDoS botnet, or the first step of a larger plan? Ceyda turns to you to determine the next step.

Ceyda: "We've found the entry point, good work. Now we need to understand what they're after. We have three options: investigate externally, focus internally, or check for spread."

Option A: Analyze the C2 Server

Research the destination IP and domain on Threat Intelligence platforms (VirusTotal, Shodan, etc.). This could provide information about the attacker's identity and TTPs.

Option B: Analyze the Payload

Analyze the downloaded 'payload.sh' script and 'c2.pl' Perl script in a sandbox. Find out exactly what the malware does and its capabilities (e.g., network scanning).

Option C: Perform an Internal Scan

Initiate an urgent scan to find other "SmartEye 3000" model cameras on the network. Understand how widespread the vulnerability is and identify other potential victims.

🕒 08:30 – Sudden Complication: Management Pressure

Just as your analysis concludes, you get that expected call from the Sales Director. His voice is tense.

Sales Director: "Is your team messing with the camera in the Ocean Room? That camera needs to be operational in less than an hour, we have a live demo for a very important international client! You can't take the room offline, find a solution immediately!"

This new information reveals that the decision is not just technical, but also operational. The pressure on the team has increased.

🏢 08:45 – Crisis Meeting and Final Decision

You brief Cybersecurity Manager Ceyda on the latest: "We have an IP camera compromised via the CVE-2025-1337 vulnerability. It's not just communicating externally, it's also acting based on the initial information we have. We haven't detected any lateral movement yet. However, the Sales Department needs that camera in less than an hour."

Ceyda: "Understood. We're short on time and under pressure. It looks like we have three options. The decision is yours. What's the play?"

As a team, make your decision:

Option A: Isolate and Patch (The Safe Route)

Immediately disconnect the camera from the network. Take the device to a lab, capture its image, and update it with the latest firmware. The threat is stopped instantly.

Risk: We won't learn more about the attacker or their ultimate goal. The sales presentation will be canceled.

Option B: Redirect to Honeypot (The Risky Intel)

Redirect the camera's traffic to a Honeypot environment, completely isolated from the main network. Secretly monitor the attacker's behavior (TTPs) to gather valuable intelligence.

Risk: The attacker might detect the Honeypot or launch a more aggressive attack (e.g., ransomware) during observation.

Option C: Quarantine and Observe (The Middle Ground)

Move the camera to an isolated VLAN with no internet access. Block its egress traffic but observe what it tries to do to 'bait' assets on the local network. This might reveal lateral movement attempts.

Risk: A configuration error could allow the attacker to escape quarantine. The sales presentation might still be delayed.

📞 09:00 – Communication Crisis: Stakeholder Management

As your technical response is being implemented, Ceyda approaches you, looking concerned. "The tech side seems to be getting under control, but my phone is ringing off the hook. The Sales Director is asking about the status of the demo, and the CFO wants a potential financial impact report. How do we manage this internally? What should our official position be?"

Ceyda: "This is as critical as the technical response. Your advice will determine how the company perceives this crisis internally. What do we tell them?"

Option A: Full Transparency

Inform the Sales Director that the demo is definitively canceled due to a critical cybersecurity incident. Brief the CFO on the full situation and advise them to prepare for a worst-case scenario.

Option B: Controlled Information

Tell Sales it's a 'temporary network issue' and you are trying to resolve it. Report to the CFO that it's an 'incident under control' to avoid causing panic.

Option C: Technical Focus Only

Say, 'My priority is to stop this threat; communication is your job.' Focus solely on the technical operation and let management handle the fallout themselves.
