Cloud Security Crisis Simulation

Welcome, **Cybersecurity Engineer** at **CORESEC Technologies**! Your company hosts its risk analysis product for the financial sector in the cloud, and the launch is just 72 hours away. However, our security team has been alerted by unusual data exfiltration requests and leak reports. It's believed that an attacker has infiltrated through an unprotected data store, a misconfigured access permission, or a disabled security step. Your mission: Stop this stealthy data leak, contain the damage, and ensure the future resilience of your cloud infrastructure!

Day 1: Data Leak Alert - 72 Hours to Launch!

09:00 AM - Call from the Security Team & Unusual Traffic

Your day starts with an urgent call from the security operations team. The Lead Analyst reports unusual data exfiltration requests from your cloud-based risk analysis product. Specifically, there's an abnormal traffic flow from the data store named `Analysis-Data-Production`. With only 72 hours until launch, every minute is critical.

The attacker is potentially exploiting:

  • A publicly exposed data store,
  • A misconfigured access permission,
  • A disabled additional security verification step,
  • Or an unprotected cloud function.

As the Cybersecurity Engineer, what is your initial response?

Learning Note: The initial moments of a crisis are critical. Will it be swift intervention or intelligence gathering? Your decision will have both technical and operational consequences.

Day 1: Afternoon - Storage Vulnerability Discovered

03:30 PM - The "Analysis-Data-Production" Data Store is Public!

Following your initial response, the team began a deep dive into the configuration of the `Analysis-Data-Production` data store. You've made a shocking discovery: the data store was accidentally set to "Public"! This means the attacker could pull data with direct requests. Furthermore, audit logs show definitive evidence that over **500 GB of data** has been exfiltrated from this store in the last 24 hours. This includes sensitive analysis algorithms and customer test data that form the core of your financial risk analysis product.

Faced with this critical finding, what is your next immediate technical step?

Learning Note: Misconfigurations are one of the biggest vulnerabilities in cloud security. Access controls for the most critical assets must be continuously audited.

Day 2: Morning - Identity and Access Catastrophe

09:30 AM - A Former Developer's Ghost Account with Full Admin Rights!

With the data store secured, you began reviewing access logs to uncover another layer of the attack. You found that the account of a former developer (`developer_cem`), who left the company months ago, is still active and, shockingly, has **full administrative privileges to all cloud resources**! This account was detected performing suspicious operations in the management panel just before accessing the `Analysis-Data-Production` data store. It appears the attacker gained initial access using this "ghost account."

How will you close this critical security vulnerability and prevent further movement by the attacker?

Learning Note: Access permissions and former account management form the foundation of cloud security. Even the smallest vulnerability can lead to full system access.

Day 2: Afternoon - Suspicious Internal Connections

02:00 PM - A Whisper of a C2 Server?

The use of the former developer's account suggests the attacker is not only exfiltrating data but also attempting to establish persistence within your internal network. In the network traffic logs, you've detected an encrypted, continuous data transfer from an internal computer (`10.0.0.42`) to an external Telegram server. This computer belongs to a developer in the Finance Department.

This situation may indicate that the attacker is using a compromised internal device as a **command and control (C2) server**. However, there's also a possibility that the developer in the Finance Department is conducting an "authorized" test or research. The CTO, Furkan Tekin, notes that his departments often conduct tests with external services.

How will you handle this suspicious internal connection?

Learning Note: Insider threats are among the most difficult to detect and manage. Wrong decisions can have devastating effects on both security and internal trust.

Day 3: Morning - Media Leak and Reputational Disaster

08:00 AM - Trending on Social Media: "Security Flaw in CORESEC Product!"

In the midst of your internal investigation, the nightmare scenario comes true: a tech journalist with a large social media following posts, "Claims of a major security flaw and customer data leak in CORESEC Technologies' highly anticipated product project!" The post quickly goes viral, and media outlets start contacting you. With only hours until the launch, CEO Dilara Akın urgently summons you to her office. Her expression is grim: *"This is a disaster! We need to make a statement immediately. Our reputation is being destroyed! What do we do?"*

Together with the PR Director and Legal Counsel, how will you manage this media crisis and what message will you convey to the public?

Learning Note: A media crisis can be one of the most devastating consequences of a cyber attack. Reputation management, transparency, speed, and the right message are key to regaining public trust.

Day 3: Afternoon - Board Meeting and Critical Decision

01:00 PM - Cancel the Launch?

In the midst of the media crisis, the board convenes for an emergency meeting. Everyone has a different priority, and tensions are high. The moment of decision has arrived, and all eyes are on you. At the end of the meeting, they present you with two options:

As the Cybersecurity Engineer, under all this pressure, what final major technical and strategic decision will you make that will determine the company's fate?

Learning Note: Critical business decisions can directly conflict with technical security risks. Finding a balance in such moments and communicating effectively with management is vital.

Day 3: Evening - Regulatory Obligations

06:00 PM - A Call from the Legal Counsel

While you are dealing with the technical and managerial aspects of the crisis, you receive a worried call from Legal Counsel Zara: *"According to the data we have, the leaked customer information qualifies as sensitive data under GDPR. This could bring us huge fines and lawsuits. We need to make an official data breach notification to the relevant authorities within 48 hours. This will directly affect our public perception and legal position. What are we doing?"*

How will you handle this legal obligation and reputational risk?

Learning Note: In data breaches, legal compliance not only avoids penalties but also reflects the company's ethical stance and customer trust.
